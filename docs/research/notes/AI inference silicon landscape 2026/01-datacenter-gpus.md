# Datacenter GPU-class accelerators for AI inference: NVIDIA, AMD, Intel (state as of 2026-10-30)

Conventions: FLOPS are **dense** unless marked "sparse"; "vendor" = vendor-published peak; "est." = third-party estimate; "historical" = fact older than ~12 months kept for baseline. Compute capability (CC) / gfx target given where known. Every number carries its source and date.

## KQ1. Specs per chip (HBM, on-chip memory, dense TFLOPS, TDP, scale-up, availability, price)

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 the shipping inference parts are NVIDIA B200/GB200 and B300/GB300 (Blackwell/Blackwell Ultra, 180–288 GB HBM3e, 8 TB/s, 9–15 PF dense FP4) and AMD MI355X (288 GB HBM3e, 8 TB/s, 10 PF FP4); NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 (288 GB HBM4, 19.2 TB/s final, 50 PF sparse NVFP4) entered production shipments in Aug–Oct 2026 with specs cut 12–17% from the preliminary targets; AMD MI455X/Helios (432 GB HBM4, 40 PF FP4) is in a slow production ramp; Intel's only new datacenter inference GPU, the D270 (ex-Crescent Island, LPDDR5X, 1.3 PF FP8), is sampling; Rubin CPX was shelved, Rubin Ultra was cut to 2 dies, and Kyber slipped to 2028.

### Inferences
- NVIDIA's "50 PF NVFP4" for Rubin is a sparse figure; the dense-equivalent inference number is not published separately (the 35 PF "training" figure is dense but may use a different datapath), so cross-vendor FP4 comparisons should use 35 PF dense Rubin vs 40.26 PF MXFP4 MI455X vs 15 PF NVFP4 B300 vs 10 PF MI355X.
- B300 dense FP4 differs by SKU: 13.5 PF (HGX B300, air) vs 15 PF (GB300 NVL72), which explains the "14 PF vs 15 PF" discrepancies in secondary sources.
- The Oct 2026 Rubin downgrade (HBM4 −12.7%, NVLink −16.7%) suggests HBM4 pin-speed and SerDes yield limits; compilers that encode roofline constants for sm_107 should treat bandwidth as configuration data, not constants.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published TDP for Rubin GPU or MI455X; no L2/SMEM/TMEM sizes for Rubin (no CUDA tuning guide for CC 10.7 as of CUDA 13.4).
- No verified list price for MI355X, MI455X, Rubin; all GPU prices are third-party estimates.
- Intel D270 memory bandwidth is only estimated (684 GB/s vs ~1.5 TB/s); process node not disclosed; no scale-up fabric disclosed.
- Jaguar Shores status after Intel's Q3 2026 call could not be determined from primary sources.
- Helios actual shipment volumes as of Oct 2026 are not disclosed (AMD says "in production"; SemiAnalysis says slow ramp).

## KQ2. Architectural features that matter most for an inference compiler, per architecture

### Takeaway
Blackwell (sm_100/103) moved the accumulator out of registers into a 256 KB per-SM Tensor Memory addressed by single-thread asynchronous `tcgen05.mma` instructions with 2-CTA (cluster-pair) tiles, block-scaled FP4/FP6/FP8 kinds, TMA with gather/scatter, and an SFU that is ~500× slower than the MMA datapath; CDNA4 kept the per-wave synchronous MFMA model but doubled matrix rate, grew LDS to 160 KB with transposed reads and 128-bit direct-to-LDS loads, and added E8M0-scaled F8F6F4 MFMAs; Intel Xe3P is a conventional XMX-per-Xe-core design with 512 KB L1/SLM per core and LPDDR5X, documented only through open-source compiler code.

### Cited Findings

**NVIDIA Hopper (baseline, CC 9.0)**
- 132 SMs, 50 MB L2, 228 KB SMEM of a 256 KB L1/SMEM pool, 64K registers/SM; clusters portable max 8 / non-portable 16; DSMEM lets blocks in a cluster read/write/atomic each other's SMEM (~7× faster than global per NVIDIA); TMA copies 1D–5D tensors global↔shared and shared↔shared across a cluster, issued by one thread with a copy descriptor; async transaction barriers count threads and bytes — [Hopper tuning guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/hopper-tuning-guide/index.html), [Hopper in-depth](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-hopper-architecture-in-depth/).
- FP8 E4M3 and E5M2 tensor cores at 2,000 TF dense; NVLink 4 900 GB/s — [Hopper in-depth](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-hopper-architecture-in-depth/).

**NVIDIA Blackwell datacenter (CC 10.0 B200, 10.3 B300)**
- Tensor Memory: 256 KB per SM = 128 lanes × 512 columns × 32-bit; address = lane in bits 31–16, column in bits 15–0; allocated in whole columns, power-of-two ≥ 32, by one warp (`tcgen05.alloc`/`dealloc`, same warp), base address shared via SMEM; `tcgen05.relinquish_alloc_permit` lets co-resident CTAs allocate; each warp of a warpgroup may access only its 32-lane quadrant (warp i → lanes 32i..32i+31); `tcgen05.ld` shapes `.16x64b/.16x128b/.16x256b/.32x32b` with `.num` x1–x128 (≤16 KB per instruction); `tcgen05.st` (regs→TMEM) and `tcgen05.cp` (SMEM→TMEM) exist with restricted patterns; TMEM exists only on CC 10.x, not consumer CC 12.0 — [Colfax 2025-04-19](https://research.colfax-intl.com/cutlass-tutorial-writing-gemm-kernels-using-tensor-memory-for-nvidia-blackwell-gpus/); TMEM = 64 KB per SM sub-partition [Chips and Cheese](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/nvidias-b200-keeping-the-cuda-juggernaut).
- UMMA (`tcgen05.mma`): operand A from SMEM or TMEM, B from SMEM, D in TMEM; a single elected thread issues it; 32-bit instruction descriptor carries dtype/sparsity/transpose/negate; `enable-input-d` toggles overwrite vs accumulate; completion via `tcgen05.commit` → mbarrier; calling `cute::gemm` from one thread without the CUTLASS election deadlocks — [Colfax](https://research.colfax-intl.com/cutlass-tutorial-writing-gemm-kernels-using-tensor-memory-for-nvidia-blackwell-gpus/).
- Shapes/kinds (CUTLASS SM100): kinds `tf32`, `f16`, `i8`, `f8f6f4`, `mxf8f6f4.block_scale`, `mxf4.block_scale`, `mxf4nvf4.block_scale` (scale_vec 2X/4X), each with `.sp` sparse and `cta_group::1|2`; 1-SM tiles M=64|128, N=64..256; 2-SM tiles M=128|256, N=64..256 (per-SM epilogue M halved); narrow-precision tile K=128 (256 for some nvf4/sparse); roughly 2–4× Hopper WGMMA throughput; largest 1-CTA BF16 UMMA 128×256×16 (~2× Hopper's wgmma atom), 2-CTA 256×256×16 — [CUTLASS Blackwell functionality](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/media/docs/cpp/blackwell_functionality.md), [Tri Dao FA4 blog](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/flash4/).
- Block scaling: D = C + (A·SFA)(B·SFB) along K; MX types use `ue8m0` scales over 32 K-elements (64 sparse), NVFP4 (`nv_float4_t`) uses `ue4m3` scales over 16 (32 sparse); scale-factor tensors are tiled in 512-byte atoms = 128 rows × 4 scales along K; alignment: nv_float4 32 elements, mx_float4 128, fp8 16; accumulator always FP32 — [CUTLASS](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/media/docs/cpp/blackwell_functionality.md). NVFP4 format: E2M1 element × E4M3 per-16 block scale × FP32 per-tensor scale, ~1.8× smaller than FP8 — [NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-blackwell-ultra-the-chip-powering-the-ai-factory-era/), [Transformer Engine docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/transformer-engine-releases/release-2.14/user-guide/features/low_precision_training/nvfp4/nvfp4.html). OCP MX v1.0: block 32, E8M0 scale; v1.1.0 (2026-10-09) renames E8M0→UE8M0 — [OCP MX v1.0](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-microscaling-formats-mx-v1-0-spec-final-pdf), [OCP MX v1.1.0](https://www.opencompute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/OCP_MicroXcaling_Spec_v1.1.0.pdf).
- Datapath asymmetry (per SM, B200): tensor cores 8,192 BF16 ops/cycle, MUFU exp 16 ops/cycle, SMEM 128 B/cycle; from H100→B200 BF16 MMA rose 1→2.25 PF while SFU count and SMEM bandwidth did not change; attention forward is exp-bound, backward SMEM-bound — [Tri Dao 2026-03](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/flash4/). Blackwell Ultra doubles SFU exp throughput: 4.5 (Hopper) → 5 (Blackwell) → 10.7 TeraExp/s — [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-blackwell-ultra-the-chip-powering-the-ai-factory-era/).
- Measured B200 memory hierarchy: L1 latency 19.6 ns (39 cycles); L2 126 MB in two partitions, ~150 ns local, slight cross-partition penalty, ~21 TB/s local vs 16.8 TB/s cross-partition bandwidth; thread-to-thread 90–100 ns same partition vs 190–220 ns cross; VRAM latency higher than H100; 1024 16-bit MACs/cycle per SM partition for CTA-level matrix ops; no double-rate FP16 vector — [Chips and Cheese 2025-12-15](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/nvidias-b200-keeping-the-cuda-juggernaut). Microbenchmark paper: [arXiv 2512.02189 (v3 2026-03-02)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02189).
- Tensor cores behave like 128×128 systolic arrays: a 64×64×64 GEMM runs at one quarter the rate of 128×128×64; CTA pairs can access each other's TMEM; Blackwell programming is "a lot more like programming a dataflow-machine" — [Hazy Research 2025-03-15](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-03-15-tk-blackwell).
- Clusters: portable 8, non-portable 16 (B200), DSMEM concurrent with L2, 32-byte aligned accesses recommended; SMEM carveouts 0–228 KB; 1 KB reserved per block — [Blackwell tuning guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/blackwell-tuning-guide/index.html).
- Target model: `sm_100` baseline, `sm_100f` family (CC 10.0 + 10.3), `sm_100a` architecture-specific; tcgen05/TMEM-class features are `a`/`f`-only; `a` code has no forward compatibility in PTX or cubin — [NVIDIA CUDA 12.9 blog 2025-05-01](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-blackwell-and-nvidia-cuda-12-9-introduce-family-specific-architecture-features/). Consumer CC 12.0 lacks TMEM, uses `mma.sync` with f8f6f4/mx kinds, cluster fixed 1×1×1, 128 KB SMEM — [CUTLASS](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/media/docs/cpp/blackwell_functionality.md), [tuning guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/blackwell-tuning-guide/index.html).
- TMA on Blackwell adds `.tile::gather4/.scatter4`, `im2col::w`, `im2col::w::128` modes, sub-byte tensor element types (`.b4x16_p64`, `.b6x16_p32`) — [PTX ISA 9.4](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html).

**NVIDIA Rubin (CC 10.7, sm_107, PTX ISA 9.4 / CUDA 13.4)**

### Inferences
- For an inference compiler the Blackwell-specific must-haves are: (1) a TMEM allocator/column-planner (power-of-two columns, per-warp lane quadrants, relinquish semantics for persistent kernels), (2) single-thread issue + mbarrier-tracked asynchronous MMA pipelines (producer/consumer warp specialization), (3) 2-CTA cluster-pair tiling with DSMEM exchange, (4) block-scale layout generation (512 B atoms, 16- vs 32-element blocks), and (5) SFU-aware scheduling (software exp emulation on FMA units as in FA4). None of these exist on CDNA3/4, where the equivalent work is LDS layout/transposition, direct-to-LDS loads, wave64 MFMA register layouts, and E8M0 scale packing.
- CDNA4 hardware scaling is MX-style (E8M0, 32-element); no evidence was found of native E4M3-scaled 16-element (NVFP4) MFMA, so NVFP4 checkpoints would need dequant/requant paths on AMD.
- Rubin's PTX additions (`fabric.*` async tensor ops, LUT decompression, UE5M3 scales, `.exclusive` TMEM allocation) indicate the ISA surface is still expanding each generation; `a`-suffixed targets mean a compiler must re-validate per CC rather than rely on forward compatibility.

### Gaps
- AMD CDNA4 ISA PDF and CDNA4 whitepaper repeatedly timed out; MFMA cycle counts, LDS bank structure, and register-file sizes for gfx950 were not verified from AMD's document (mnemonics taken from LLVM tests).
- No public microarchitecture data for Rubin (SM count, L2, TMEM size) or MI455X CU-level changes (CDNA "next" details not published).
- Intel has not published an Xe3P ISA or memory-latency data; XMX instruction shapes for Xe3P are not documented outside compiler source.
- LLVM NVPTX documentation of `llvm.nvvm.tcgen05.*` intrinsics could not be confirmed in the fetched portions of the page.

## KQ3. Openness to a third-party open-source compiler and lowest practical entry point

### Takeaway
AMD is the only vendor where an independent compiler can legally and practically emit native machine code end-to-end (public CDNA ISA PDFs + upstream LLVM AMDGPU + open HIP/ROCr); NVIDIA's lowest legal entry is PTX text (or NVPTX LLVM IR) handed to the closed `ptxas`/driver JIT, with the new Apache-licensed CUDA Tile IR as a second, Blackwell-only, closed-backend path and SASS undocumented beyond opcode names; Intel's entry is SPIR-V into the open-source (MIT) IGC/compute-runtime stack, but Xe2/Xe3 native ISA manuals are unpublished and the native `zebin` format is unstable, so the native ISA is reachable only by reading/modifying IGC.

## KQ4. Vendor inference software roadmaps: what they emphasize

### Takeaway
All three vendors now frame inference as a distributed-systems problem: NVIDIA ships Dynamo (Rust/Python, Apache-2.0) for disaggregated prefill/decode, KV-aware routing and multi-tier KV offload, pairs Rubin with Groq 3 LPX for attention–FFN disaggregation, pushes NVFP4 and MTP everywhere, and collapsed TensorRT-LLM to a PyTorch-only engine; AMD's roadmap is ROCm 10 on a 6-week cadence, AITER/CK kernels, OCP MX formats, upstream vLLM/SGLang with PD disaggregation, and the Helios open rack; Intel positions the D-Series as a memory-capacity inference card riding other vendors' serving stacks (vLLM, SGLang, llm-d, Dynamo).

### Cited Findings
- Dynamo: "open-source, datacenter-scale inference stack" above vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM; disaggregated prefill/decode, KV-aware routing, KV Block Manager tiering across GPU/CPU/SSD/remote (KVBM "in progress" on TRT-LLM), NIXL transfers, SLA-driven Planner, Grove topology scheduling, canary health checks with in-flight migration; "Built in Rust for performance, Python for extensibility"; Apache-2.0; containers tagged 1.6.0; Dynamo 1.0 launched March 2026 — [ai-dynamo/dynamo](https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo). Roadmap 1.5 (2026-09-16), 1.6 (2026-10-21), 1.7 (2026-11-18): encode/prefill/decode (EPD) disaggregation, DRAM-to-DRAM distributed KV cache then SSD/remote tiers, Sidecar disaggregated serving, SLA-aware heterogeneous routing, multi-node Elastic EP with NVLink/NVL72 locality, cross-pod MNNVL, runtime-free Router — [Dynamo roadmap issue #14124](https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo/issues/14124). ## KQ6. Where NVIDIA's moat lies today, per credible analyses

### Takeaway
Credible 2026 analyses converge on: the moat has moved up from single-GPU CUDA kernels to (a) rack-scale co-design — NVLink/NVSwitch with in-network reduction, passive backplanes, co-designed NICs — and (b) software velocity and distributed-inference composability (wide EP, disaggregation, KV transport, context parallelism) backed by large internal GPU/CI fleets; SemiAnalysis simultaneously argues AI agents and manufacturing slips (Rubin Ultra, Kyber) are eroding the kernel-level and execution advantages, while its own AgentX data shows the serving-stack gap is still decisive.

### Cited Findings
- "Speed is the moat": AMD chips can be "extremely competitive with Nvidia" if AMD "could move faster on the software front" — [SemiAnalysis 2026-02-16](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencex-v2-nvidia-blackwell-vs).
- The moat "is shifting from single-node kernels to distributed inference: WideEP, disaggregated prefill/decode, KV transport, routing, and scheduling"; NVIDIA advantages: co-designed NVSwitch (bandwidth divided evenly across 72 GPUs vs AMD's merchant Tomahawk 6), passive cableless Oberon backplane vs Helios flyover cables with retimers, "large stable internal GPU clusters"; agents reduce the CUDA headcount advantage; AMD has a "great chance" if rack production and CI capacity are fixed — [SemiAnalysis 2026-07-25](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing).

## KQ7. Recognized unsolved problems and missing pieces, in practitioners' words

### Takeaway
Practitioners name five open problems: hardware asymmetry (MMA vs exp vs SMEM bandwidth) that forces algorithm–pipeline co-design; TMEM/asynchrony semantics that no high-level compiler (Triton, TileIR backend) yet captures without escape hatches; composability of disaggregation, wide EP, FP4 and KV offload across engines; CI/silicon access for anyone outside the vendor; and documentation/ISA gaps (SASS, Xe2/Xe3, CDNA-next) that keep third-party backends one generation behind.

### Cited Findings
- Asymmetric scaling: tensor throughput 2.25× from H100 to B200 "while SFU count and shared memory bandwidth stay unchanged"; forward attention bound by compute *and* exp, backward by SMEM; FA4's answer is software exp emulation (Cody-Waite + cubic Horner, split tunable per row) and conditional rescaling — [Tri Dao](https://tridao.me/blog/2026/flash4/).
- Programming model: "Keeping the tensor cores fed is the central challenge"; Blackwell is "a lot more like programming a dataflow-machine" — [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-03-15-tk-blackwell). High performance needs "hard to discover" patterns: single-thread MMA issuance via leader election, multi-barrier pipelining, TMEM allocation with contention handling; "Every one of those primitives is discoverable. The specific combination... is not" — [Toulme](https://patricktoulme.substack.com/p/cutile-on-blackwell-nvidias-compiler).
- Formal semantics: challenges in "formalizing Blackwell's managed tensor memory, descriptor-based operand layouts, and asynchronous execution (commits, waits, memory barriers, proxy fences)" — [arXiv 2609.36800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36800).
- Compiler abstraction limits: Triton "struggles with the newest hardware features like FP8 and TMA", "doesn't abstract things like pipelining and async memory transfers" — [Modular](https://www.modular.com/blog/democratizing-ai-compute-part-7-what-about-triton-and-python-edsls); Gluon exists because "high-level abstractions couldn't find the right codegen" (secondary interpretation) — [Toulme](https://patricktoulme.substack.com/p/cutile-on-blackwell-nvidias-compiler); Triton-to-TileIR "not all Triton operations are implemented", tensor-of-pointer slow — [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/advancing-gpu-programming-with-the-cuda-tile-ir-backend-for-openai-triton/).
- Resource-protocol bugs as a class: TMEM "is not allocated" faults, missing allocations, deadlocks from alloc/relinquish misuse, over-counted `expect_tx` bytes, fences omitted on copy ops, alignment rules that change with CUDA minor versions — [DeepGEMM #479](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepGEMM/issues/479), [cutlass #2230](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/issues/2230), [tilelang #2644](https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/issues/2644); NVIDIA's own recommendation is a runtime checker (`--g-tensor-memory-access-check`) — [Colfax](https://research.colfax-intl.com/cutlass-tutorial-writing-gemm-kernels-using-tensor-memory-for-nvidia-blackwell-gpus/); TMEM is a shared, unvirtualized per-SM resource that libraries and user kernels can corrupt across each other — [CUDA 13.4 known issues](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/13.4.1/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/index.html).
- Serving composability: "disagg prefill, wide EP, and FP4 do not combine well" on AMD, "the biggest issue"; prefill/decode workers must share model, dtype, block size and KV layout or produce corrupt output — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencex-v2-nvidia-blackwell-vs), [Dynamo docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/dynamo/v-0-7-1/design-docs/disaggregated-serving); Dynamo's own roadmap still lists multi-node Elastic EP, SSD/remote KV tiers, a transfer-neutral storage boundary and SLA-aware heterogeneous routing as future work (1.6–1.7, Oct–Nov 2026) — [Dynamo #14124](https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo/issues/14124); benchmarks still use random single-turn data with prefix caching disabled, and GSM8k-only accuracy checks ("saturated") — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencex-v2-nvidia-blackwell-vs).
- CI and silicon access: AMD needs "many more MI300/MI325/MI355X CI machines"; vLLM has "no MI355X CI tests" — [SemiAnalysis Feb 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/inferencex-v2-nvidia-blackwell-vs); AMD's two gating risks are rack production and "internal GPU/CI capacity" — [SemiAnalysis Jul 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/can-amd-break-the-cuda-moat-amd-advancing); open-source Blackwell backends audited without any sm_100 hardware — [tilelang #2644](https://github.com/tile-ai/tilelang/issues/2644).
- Documentation/ISA gaps: SASS encodings undocumented and no assembler — [CUDA Binary Utilities](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-binary-utilities/index.html); Rubin platform support "developer preview… not intended for benchmarking or production" in CUDA 13.4 — [release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/13.4.1/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/index.html); no Xe2/Xe3 PRMs — [Intel Community](https://community.intel.com/t5/Graphics/Xe2-Programmer-s-Reference-Manual/td-p/1708752); LLVM lists gfx950/gfx1250 products as "TBA" and no MI400/CDNA-next ISA document exists on AMD's architecture page — [LLVM](https://llvm.org/docs/AMDGPUUsage.html), [ROCm docs](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/en/latest/reference/gpu-arch/index.html).
- Portability: Triton code tuned for one GPU "often runs poorly on others, even across NVIDIA hardware"; AMD "feature parity lag far behind" — [Modular](https://www.modular.com/blog/democratizing-ai-compute-part-7-what-about-triton-and-python-edsls); PTX `a`-suffixed features have "no forward compatibility" — [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-blackwell-and-nvidia-cuda-12-9-introduce-family-specific-architecture-features/).
- Hardware roadmap volatility: specs published during development were "preliminary architectural targets" (NVIDIA on Rubin) — [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-official-statement-on-vera-rubin-spec-change/); Rubin CPX removed six months after announcement — [The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=10763).

### Inferences
- The field lacks an open, verified intermediate layer between "tile-level DSL" and "PTX/ISA" that models asynchronous resources (TMEM, mbarrier phases, TMA transaction counts, proxy fences, cluster pairs) with static checking; the AI-as-compiler paper's PTX verifier and NVIDIA's runtime TMEM checker are the only tooling found. A Rust compiler could differentiate by making these protocols typed and checkable at compile time and by emitting portable serving-layer contracts (KV block layout, dtype, transport).
- Cross-vendor FP4 is not one format: NVFP4 (E4M3/16) vs MXFP4 (UE8M0/32); a portable quantization IR must carry block size and scale dtype explicitly.

### Gaps
- No first-hand quotes from AMD or Intel kernel engineers on CDNA4/Xe3P compiler difficulties were found (AMD sources are analysts and vLLM blog authors; Intel sources are forum threads).
- No public data on how often NVIDIA's `ptxas` changes codegen for existing PTX across 13.x minor releases (relevant to a PTX-emitting compiler's performance stability) beyond the single Triton/ptxas miscompile report.
